The red light from the terminal cast an eerie glow across Morpheus's face as he scrolled through the code. His skeletal fingers danced across the keyboard, the bones illuminated beneath translucent skin. Nobody questioned his methods anymore. Not after what happened to Neo.

"Two years," he muttered. "Two years is the window."

Tank shifted uncomfortably behind him. "Sir, the Thompson extraction is scheduled for tonight. Her neural patterns are optimal. Why postpone?"

Morpheus didn't turn from the screen, his eyes fixed on the cascading green symbols. "Because I said so."

This was the new Morpheus. Cold. Calculating. Ever since the Oracle had revealed the Temporal Paradox Theory, he had become obsessed with precision. The theory suggested that each human had a specific twenty-four month window when their mind was most receptive to awakening. Extract them too early, their consciousness would reject reality. Extract them too late, and the Matrix would have too strong a hold.

Aria watched the woman with fiery hair through the café window. Jessica Thompson. A programmer with a knack for recognizing patterns in the code. She was showing all the signs - déjà vu, questioning reality, sensing the glitches. The perfect candidate.

But Morpheus had said no. Wait another six months.

"She's ready now," Aria argued into her comm link. "I can see it in her eyes. That look of someone who knows something's wrong with the world."

"The window isn't right," Morpheus's voice crackled back. "The Oracle's prediction was specific. Extract her now, and she'll go insane. Just like the others."

Aria shuddered, remembering the wall of photographs in Morpheus's quarters. Faces of those extracted too early. Their minds had rejected the truth, their bodies had withered away. Some had even torn their own eyes out, unable to bear seeing the desolation of the real world.

Jessica suddenly looked up, as if sensing Aria's gaze. For a moment, their eyes locked through the window. Jessica's brow furrowed. She recognized something in Aria - a glitch in her own personal Matrix.

"I'm extracting her," Aria decided. "Tonight."

"You defy me?" Morpheus's voice was deadly calm.

"I'm saving her," Aria replied, switching off her comm.

## The Awakening

The extraction went smoothly. Too smoothly. Jessica Thompson took the red pill without hesitation, her fingers trembling as she lifted it to her lips.

"You feel it, don't you?" Aria had said. "That something's wrong with the world."

"All my life," Jessica had whispered back.

As Jessica's body convulsed in the chair, the extraction programs humming around her, Aria felt a moment of doubt. What if Morpheus was right? What if the window truly mattered?

When Jessica opened her eyes in the real world, screaming at the sight of the insertion needles, Aria was there to hold her hand. The woman's terror subsided as Aria explained everything - the machines, the Matrix, the war. Jessica absorbed it all with remarkable calm.

"I always knew," she said weakly. "I just didn't have the words for it."

For three days, Jessica Thompson showed all signs of a successful extraction. She ate the protein mush without complaint. She asked intelligent questions. She even laughed at Tank's terrible jokes.

Morpheus watched from a distance, his dark glasses hiding his eyes. "Wait," was all he said.

On the fourth day, Jessica began to scratch at her arms. Small at first, then with increasing violence. By nightfall, she was convinced that machines were crawling beneath her skin.

"I can feel them," she screamed as they restrained her. "The code! It's still inside me!"

"Six months," Morpheus said, standing over Jessica's sedated body. "That's how early you pulled her. Six months before her window."

Aria couldn't look at Jessica's self-inflicted wounds. "How could you possibly know that with such precision?"

Morpheus removed his glasses, revealing eyes that seemed to hold the weight of hundreds of failed extractions.

"Because I've been testing the theory for decades. Keeping records. Watching patterns. The human mind is synchronized with the Matrix more deeply than we understood. Extract someone outside their window, and the results are... catastrophic."

He gestured to the medical bay where others like Jessica lay in various states of psychosis. Some babbled endlessly about seeing code everywhere. Others had gone completely catatonic.

"The Matrix isn't just a prison for the body," Morpheus continued. "It's a developmental system for the mind. Extract a mind too early, before it's ready to question its reality, and it breaks. Extract it too late, after it's fully integrated, and it rejects the truth entirely."

Aria looked at the monitors displaying Jessica's neural patterns. They were erratic, fragmented. "Can we help her?"

"There's one option," Morpheus said grimly. "We can put her back."

Reintegration into the Matrix was risky. The machines would detect the anomaly - a mind that had been freed trying to return to its prison. But Morpheus had done it before, in the early days of testing his theory.

"The process will erase her memories of the real world," he explained. "She'll wake up in her apartment, believing she had a mental breakdown. The Matrix will adjust her life trajectory slightly to account for the lost time."

"And in six months?" Aria asked.

"If the window theory is correct, we extract her again. And this time, her mind will be ready."

Aria looked at Jessica's ravaged face. "This is why they call you cruel, Morpheus. This is why crews request transfers away from the Nebuchadnezzar."

Morpheus didn't flinch. "I'm not cruel. I'm right. And every mind we save properly is proof of that."

He laid a hand on Jessica's forehead. "She'll live a normal life for six more months. That's more than most of us ever got."

Aria turned away, unable to watch as they prepared Jessica for reintegration. The machines would accept her back - they always did. The prison was designed to hold minds, not reject them.

"What about the others?" she asked quietly. "The ones we pulled too late?"

Morpheus's voice was flat. "They become agents. Their minds so thoroughly integrated with the Matrix that they defend it, even knowing it's a lie."

Six months later, Aria stood in the same café, watching Jessica Thompson through the window. Her hair was still fiery red, but now there was a bandage around her wrist - a suicide attempt that the Matrix had manufactured to explain her mental break.

Jessica was typing furiously on her laptop, her eyes occasionally darting up to scan the café. She was seeing them now - the glitches, the patterns, the déjà vu moments that signaled her mind was ready to awaken.

"Her window is open," Morpheus's voice came through the comm link. "You were right about her potential. Just wrong about the timing."

Aria watched as Jessica suddenly stared at her own reflection in the laptop screen, touching it as if trying to determine if it was real.

"We'll extract her tonight," Morpheus continued. "And this time, she'll survive the awakening."

"Does she know?" Aria asked. "Somewhere deep in her mind, does she remember what we did to her?"

There was a long pause before Morpheus answered. "That's the real cruelty of the Matrix, Aria. Not my methods. The real cruelty is that we have to break people to save them. And sometimes, breaking them means waiting until they're ready to be broken."

As if on cue, Jessica looked up from her laptop and stared directly at Aria. This time, there was recognition in her eyes - not of Aria specifically, but of what she represented: an anomaly, a glitch, a way out.

Aria raised her hand, showing Jessica the unmarked white pill in her palm. A signal, a promise. Tonight, they would meet again. And this time, when Jessica took the red pill, her mind would be ready for the truth.

Morpheus had been right. The timing was everything.
